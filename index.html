I build low-level systems and the mathematics behind them.

I'm Faris, a systems developer and self-proclaimed mathematician. I'm building planckOS, a from-scratch x86_64 operating system, and Plasma, the quantum language and deterministic simulator that runs inside it. I start a Mathematics degree in 2027.

Most of my work sits close to the metal: bare-metal firmware, bootloaders, kernels, fixed-point arithmetic, and deterministic simulation. Away from the kernel, I play competitive Minecraft 1.8.9 PvP and moderate for Voxyl Network. I also build small exact maths tools for A-level and undergraduate study.

03Projects

Things I'm building because I want to understand the bottom of the stack, starting with Plasma, the quantum language, and the operating system that ships it.

Plasma

A high-level quantum computing language and deterministic statevector simulator, designed for planckOS. Line-based assembly-like syntax, reproducible output per seed, and backends from a reference interpreter to Clifford stabilizer, MPS, and a PTX-only CUDA path.

  • Rust
  • Quantum
  • Simulation
  • Deterministic
2026

planckOS

A Rust-based quantum simulation OS being built entirely from scratch: custom MBR and loader, 64-bit kernel, FAT32 driver, SMP, and a REPL shell, with Plasma shipped on every release.

  • Rust
  • Assembly
  • Kernel
  • FAT32
  • SMP
2026

NumCore

Bare-metal scientific calculator firmware for the ARM Cortex-M3 in Rust #![no_std], with no OS, no allocator, and no standard library, plus a deterministic Q31.32 fixed-point math engine.

  • Rust
  • no_std
  • Cortex-M3
  • Fixed-Point
2025

Tungsten

A from-scratch mathematical platform for symbolic algebra, exact calculus, and N-dimensional graphing, written in zero-dependency Rust. A FOSS alternative to Mathematica, WIP.

  • Rust
  • Mathematics
  • Symbolic Algebra
  • WIP
2026

μT

A terminal-based LaTeX editor with Micro-like editing, asynchronous pdflatex compilation, Zathura preview, and syntax highlighting.

05Research

  • 2026 – Present

    Invariant Detection: A Theory of Cross-Packet Invariants

    Ongoing research developing a theory of invariant detection and cumulative residuals, with a synthetic Minecraft anti-cheat experiment and the mc-trace/v1 trace toolkit. The work includes a manuscript, an R experiment, and a trace specification.

  • Apr 2026 – Aug 2026

    AI Systems and Security

    Supervised research under the University of Manchester's Manchester Access Programme. It asks how publicly accessible large language models can be exploited for cybercrime such as phishing, malware development, and social engineering, and whether the same models are becoming the most effective defensive tool available, from threat detection to automated vulnerability assessment.
